I'm Yun Yue Zhao — a Senior Consultant in Deloitte's Finance Transformation practice, engaged with a real estate asset management platform at a major global financial institution. My work sits at the intersection of real estate fund finance, operating-model modernization, and the AI-enabled tooling reshaping how institutional investors run their finance functions.

The trajectory wasn't accidental. Each move was made with the next one already partially in view.

01Foundation

I started at the University of Washington in biochemistry. A foundation in the natural sciences teaches you to read complex systems patiently, to design experiments that isolate variables, and to trust precise measurement over confident assertion. Those are habits I've carried into every chair since.

The pivot into finance and accounting came during undergrad. I earned a Master of Accountancy at the University of Denver, then secured CPA licenses in California and Colorado — credentials chosen for the doors they open into analytical, consequential financial work.

02Audit

The next five years were spent in Big 4 audit — across investment funds, banks, and capital markets clients.

At Plante Moran, I was an Audit Senior in the investment fund practice — engagements spanning real estate funds, private equity funds, and alternative investment vehicles. I supervised junior staff, drafted engagement budgets averaging $100K–$300K, and took on manager-level responsibilities on select engagements ahead of my title. The deeper value of those years wasn't the management reps — it was the working fluency in fund-level mechanics: capital account roll-forwards, management fee and carried interest calculations, distribution waterfalls, side letter compliance. Mechanics learned by auditing the books and records of dozens of fund vehicles, including real estate, private equity, and alternative investment funds.

At PwC, I moved into the Banking & Capital Markets practice in Denver, working with financial institutions across credit, real estate lending, and structured finance portfolios. I executed substantive audit procedures on high-risk line items — loan confirmations, allowance for credit losses, collateral valuations — and coordinated a 20+ person delivery team across India and Argentina through 24-hour delivery cycles. I built audit automation workflows using Alteryx and PwC's Digital Lab tools, driving efficiency gains of up to 20% across engagements.

03Tokyo

In 2023 I moved to Tokyo for an MBA at Waseda University — strategic framing, cross-border relationships, and the time to think clearly about where the audit foundation should compound next. The answer that emerged: toward the investment side.

The MBA was complemented by six months on the hedge fund desk at Asian Technology Advisors — research on 15+ global companies across semiconductors, technology, and automotive, supporting senior-analyst coverage decisions on portfolios ranging from $200M to $10B AUM. The buy-side seat teaches the inverse of the audit seat: not how financial systems work internally, but how markets price what those systems produce.

The Tokyo chair also developed the thematic lens I carry into real estate. The AI infrastructure capex cycle I covered — Asia-Pacific semiconductor leaders, hyperscaler customers — sits directly upstream of the data center demand drivers now central to digital-infrastructure REIT and data-center investment theses.

04Deloitte · the chair I'm in now

I joined Deloitte's Finance Transformation practice in December 2025. The choice was deliberate.

The engagement I'm staffed on is a $2M+ annual transformation supporting the real estate asset management platform of a major global financial institution — direct RE AUM across multiple capital vehicles spanning core through development strategies, with global operations. From that seat I'm contributing to NAV preparation, fund-level reporting, intercompany reconciliations, and capital call / distribution mechanics; helping diagnose and redesign recurring finance processes; identifying and scoping 12 AI and automation use cases with implementation plans for 4 prioritized initiatives; and developing materials for the steering committees governing the transformation roadmap.

Three things make this seat unusually valuable for where I want to go next.

First, the technical content is real. I'm working directly alongside the people who run institutional real estate finance — every conversation about NAV preparation, fund-level reporting, intercompany reconciliations, or capital call mechanics is one I'll have on the other side of the table once I move to an investment seat.

Second, the AI-enablement work is differentiated. Few traditional REPE candidates have working experience deploying AI in regulated finance contexts; few AI-fluent candidates have real estate domain fluency. The intersection is one institutional platforms are increasingly hiring against.

Third, the relationships are real. The working relationships I'm building on the investment side of the engagement are the kind of network capital no formal pipeline can replace.

05Beyond

The destination I'm building toward is a senior associate or VP role in institutional real estate investment management — via internal transition to the investment side of the current platform, or to a comparable institutional RE platform. That's a three-to-five-year build, not a two-year sprint. The development plan is mapped: real estate financial modeling (ARGUS, deal underwriting), sector thesis depth (data centers and industrial), CFA Level III and charter completion, AI differentiation, network capital, and deal-adjacent experience.

Each year, the picture clarifies. The current chair is the one I'd choose all over again.

I hold CPA licenses in California and Colorado, an MBA from Waseda University, a Master of Accountancy from the University of Denver, and am pursuing CFA Level III. I work across English, Mandarin, and Japanese, and have lived and worked across the U.S., Japan, and China. I'm based in Costa Mesa, California.
