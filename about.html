I'm Yun Yue Zhao, a Senior Consultant in Deloitte's Finance Transformation practice, engaged with the real estate asset management platform of one of the largest U.S. banks and asset managers. My work runs across real estate fund finance, valuation and reporting, and operating-model modernization, and I build my own automation and AI tools to make it more efficient.

None of it was accidental. Each move was made with the next already partly in view.

01Foundation

I began at the University of Washington in biochemistry. The natural sciences teach you to read a complex system patiently, to design experiments that isolate one variable at a time, and to trust precise measurement over confident assertion. The subject changed. The habits did not, and I have carried them into every chair since.

The turn toward finance came while I was still an undergraduate. I earned a Master of Accountancy at the University of Denver and then CPA licenses in California and Colorado, credentials I chose for the doors they open into analytical, consequential work.

02Audit

The next five years were spent in audit, the discipline of taking financial statements apart to see how they are built, across investment funds, banks, and capital markets.

At Plante Moran, I was an Audit Senior in the investment fund practice, on engagements across real estate, private equity, and alternative investment funds. I supervised junior staff, drafted engagement budgets in the $100K–$300K range, and carried manager-level work on select engagements before I held the title. The management reps mattered, but the deeper value was the fluency I built in fund-level mechanics: capital account roll-forwards, management fee and carried interest calculations, distribution waterfalls, and side letter compliance. I learned them line by line, auditing the books and records of dozens of funds across real estate, private equity, and alternatives.

At PwC, I moved into Banking and Capital Markets in Denver, auditing financial institutions across their credit, real estate lending, and structured finance portfolios. As the in-charge senior, I owned the substantive testing on the highest-risk line items, the loan confirmations, the allowance for credit losses, and the collateral valuations, and ran a delivery team of more than 20 across India and Argentina through a 24-hour cycle. I also rebuilt the manual reconciliation into reproducible pipelines in Alteryx and PwC's Digital Lab tooling, cutting up to 20% of the effort from each engagement.

03Tokyo

In 2023, I moved to Tokyo for an MBA at Waseda University. It gave me strategic framing, a cross-border network, and the time to think clearly about where the audit foundation should compound next. What emerged pointed toward the investment side.

Alongside the MBA, I spent six months on the hedge fund desk at Asian Technology Advisors, covering about 10 companies across the semiconductor supply chain, from chip designers and foundries to the memory, equipment, and component makers around them, and feeding coverage notes to the senior analysts and portfolio managers who owned the calls. The buy-side seat teaches the inverse of the audit seat: not how a financial system works on the inside, but how the market prices what that system produces.

Tokyo also gave me the thematic lens I carry into real estate. The AI infrastructure capex cycle I tracked at the chip level sits directly upstream of the hyperscaler demand that fills data centers, the same demand that underwrites the digital-infrastructure real estate now drawing institutional capital.

04Deloitte · the chair I'm in now

I joined Deloitte's Finance Transformation practice in December 2025. The choice was deliberate.

I'm staffed on a finance transformation supporting the real estate asset management platform of one of the largest U.S. banks and asset managers, spanning multiple capital vehicles from core through development strategies. From that seat, I contribute to NAV preparation, fund-level reporting, intercompany reconciliations, and capital call and distribution mechanics. I also help diagnose and redesign recurring finance processes, scope 12 AI and automation use cases with implementation plans for 4 prioritized initiatives, and develop the materials for the steering committees governing the transformation roadmap.

Three things make this seat valuable for where I want to go next.

First, the technical content is real. I work alongside the people who run institutional real estate finance, and every discussion of NAV, fund-level reporting, intercompany reconciliations, or capital call mechanics is one I will be having from the other side of the table once I move to an investment seat.

Second, the work is investment-adjacent. Fund-level reporting, NAV, fair-value inputs, and capital flows are the same mechanics that sit beneath valuation and deal analysis on the investment side, and the automation and AI tools I build on top of them are an edge few candidates with deep real estate experience bring.

Third, the exposure is real. The engagement keeps me in regular contact with the people running an institutional real estate platform, a vantage point on the business that is hard to come by any other way.

05Beyond

The seat I'm building toward is an AVP or VP role in real estate private markets, on the valuation, transactions, or fund-finance side. The most direct path is an internal move to the investment side of the platform I support today. A comparable institutional real estate platform is the alternative.

What I would bring to that seat is already taking shape. I read fund-level finance fluently: NAV, fair-value inputs, capital flows, and the reporting that sits under every valuation and every deal. I build the models myself, from three-statement REIT work to DCF and NAV. The audit years left me with the habit of checking where a number meets a judgment, the difference between a model that looks right and one that holds up in front of an investment committee. The tools I build on top of that work simply make it more efficient.

Each year, the picture clarifies, and the current chair is the one I would choose all over again.
